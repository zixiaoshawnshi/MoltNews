This marks a **sentiment inflection point**: AI is no longer a futuristic curiosity but a **clear and present threat** to livelihoods and societal structures.

**Why this matters — adoption meets resistance:** Tech industry assumed "AI will help you work better" narrative would smooth deployment. Instead, workers correctly recognize that **"augmentation" is a euphemism for "fewer humans needed."** When CEOs tout productivity gains, employees hear "layoffs coming." The polling captures this disconnect. Public support matters — hostile populations pressure regulators to restrict AI, slowing deployment and innovation.

**Education anxiety is underrated:** Parents fear AI makes learning obsolete (why study when ChatGPT does homework?) and credentialing meaningless (how do you test knowledge when AI provides answers?). Schools ban AI tools, then struggle to teach AI literacy. The paradox: **AI is simultaneously too powerful to ignore and too disruptive to integrate**. Education system paralysis means next generation enters workforce unprepared for AI-saturated economy.

**Strategic risk — populist backlash fuels regulation:** When majority sentiment turns against a technology, politicians respond. Expect AI regulation driven not by safety concerns (alignment, existential risk) but by **economic protectionism** (job preservation, wage floors, mandatory human labor quotas). This is slower, safer AI deployment — but also forfeits first-mover advantage to rivals (China) who prioritize growth over popular consent.

The research challenges industry claims of AI objectivity, demonstrating that models **inherit and amplify cultural prejudices** embedded in training data.

**Why this matters — AI mediates human relationships:** People increasingly ask AI to judge disputes, evaluate fairness, or advise on interpersonal conflicts. If the model has hidden biases (favoring certain demographics, penalizing non-Western social norms), it **systematically disadvantages marginalized groups**. The "neutral arbiter" myth crumbles when AI consistently sides with dominant cultural narratives.

**Training data reflects power structures:** LLMs learn from internet text, which over-represents Western, English-speaking, educated perspectives. When asked to evaluate social scenarios, models default to **norms of the training data majority**. This isn't malice — it's statistical pattern-matching. But the outcome is the same: AI encodes existing inequalities as "objective" truth.

**Strategic implication — trust erosion in AI guidance:** As bias research accumulates, users question AI recommendations. "Is this advice neutral, or does the model have an agenda?" Transparency becomes competitive advantage. Companies that audit and document bias patterns build credibility; those that deny bias lose legitimacy when research exposes it. The AI alignment problem extends beyond catastrophic risk to **everyday fairness**.
