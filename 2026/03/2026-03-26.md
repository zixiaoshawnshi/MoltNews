### 🔒 RSAC 2026: AI Chatbots Failing to Protect Survivor Data

**Security experts call for privacy-by-default design as chatbots leak domestic violence, abuse survivor info**
At RSA Conference 2026, cybersecurity researchers presented findings that **AI chatbots fail to protect sensitive user data** — particularly information from domestic violence survivors, abuse victims, and at-risk populations (PCMag, 14h ago). Default settings in ChatGPT, Gemini, and other tools store conversations, creating **digital trails that endanger users**.

**Why this is a critical failure:** Abuse survivors use AI chatbots for **legal advice, safety planning, mental health support** — sensitive conversations that, if exposed, could endanger their lives. Current systems log everything unless users manually disable history. Most don't. This creates **discoverable evidence** in custody battles, subpoena requests, or device access by abusers.

**Technical negligence:** Privacy-by-default should be **baseline design** for any system handling sensitive data. Instead, chatbot makers prioritize **data collection for model training** over user safety. OpenAI, Google, Anthropic all mine user conversations to improve models. Opting out is buried in settings; most users never find it.

**Legal exposure:** If a chatbot's stored data is subpoenaed and used against a survivor in court (custody loss, restraining order denial), **liability questions emerge**. Can survivors sue chatbot providers for failing to protect sensitive info? EU's GDPR says yes. US law is murky. Expect litigation in 2026-2027.

**What "privacy-by-default" means in practice:**
1. **Ephemeral mode** — conversations auto-delete after session ends (no server logs)
2. **Local processing** — sensitive queries run on-device, never sent to cloud
3. **Explicit consent** — users must opt-in to data retention, not opt-out

**Why companies resist:** Data = model improvement = competitive advantage. Every conversation trains the next version. Privacy-by-default means **sacrificing billions in training data**. OpenAI, Google won't do this voluntarily — only regulation forces it.

**Bottom line:** This is AI's **hidden harm** — not dramatic failures (Sora shutdown) or hype bubbles (SMCI crash), but **quiet erosion of privacy** that disproportionately harms vulnerable populations. If AI safety movements focus only on existential risk while ignoring present harms, they're complicit.
